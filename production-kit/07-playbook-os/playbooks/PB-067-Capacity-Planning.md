# PB-067 --- Capacity Planning

> **Module:** 09-enterprise-execution **Playbook ID:** PB-067
> **Version:** 1.0.0 **Status:** Stable

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Executive Summary

Establish a standardized Capacity Planning framework to forecast,
assess, balance, and optimize enterprise AI capacity across people,
infrastructure, platforms, data, and operational capabilities.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Purpose

Ensure sufficient capacity is available to meet current and future AI
demand while optimizing utilization, minimizing bottlenecks, and
supporting sustainable growth.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Business Value

-   Improve delivery predictability.
-   Balance demand and supply.
-   Reduce operational bottlenecks.
-   Support scalable AI growth.
-   Optimize infrastructure investments.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Prerequisites

-   PB-066 Resource Management completed.

# Capacity Planning Lifecycle

1.  Forecast business demand.
2.  Assess current capacity.
3.  Identify capacity gaps.
4.  Evaluate response options.
5.  Allocate additional capacity.
6.  Monitor utilization.
7.  Adjust plans continuously.
8.  Review planning assumptions.

# Decision Rules

  Condition                    Action
  ---------------------------- -----------------------------------------
  Capacity shortage            Increase resources or reprioritize work
  Sustained underutilization   Optimize or reduce capacity
  Demand spike                 Activate contingency plan
  Strategic expansion          Update long-term capacity forecast

# Best Practices

-   Review forecasts monthly.
-   Maintain contingency capacity.
-   Use historical utilization trends.
-   Align planning with strategic priorities.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Common Mistakes

-   Planning only for short-term demand.
-   Ignoring skill shortages.
-   Overcommitting shared resources.
-   Failing to revise forecasts.
